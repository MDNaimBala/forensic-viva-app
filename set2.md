### Question 04: Classify antidotes.

**Answer:**

**Classification of antidotes:**  
1. **Physical or mechanical antidote:** Prevent action of poison mechanically, without destroying or inactivating damaging actions of poisons [8].  
   * *Examples:* Demulcents (egg albumin, starch, barley water, milk), Adsorbents (activated animal charcoal), Diluents (water, milk), Bulky food (boiled rice, boiled vegetables) [8].  

2. **Chemical antidote:** Disintegrate and inactivate poisons by undergoing chemical reaction with them [8].  
   * *Examples:* Weak non-carbonate alkalies for acids, weak vegetable acids for alkalies, common salt for silver nitrate, egg albumin for mercuric chloride, freshly prepared ferric oxide for arsenic, $\text{KMnO}_4$ for opium and other alkaloids [9].  

3. **Physiological or pharmacological antidote:** Have their own action on different systems of body, producing signs and symptoms opposite to those produced by poisons [9].  
   * *Examples:* Naloxone for morphine, Neostigmine for Datura or hyoscine group, Barbiturate for strychnine [10].  

4. **Chelating agents:** Act on absorbed metallic poisons by forming chelate with metallic poisons freely available in circulation, helping early excretion through kidney [10].  
   * *Examples:* Dimercaprol (BAL), EDTA, Penicillamine (cuprimine), Desferrioxamine [10].  

5. **Serological antidote:**  
   * *Example:* Anti-snake venom serum (AVS) [10].

### Question 09: Why ultra-sonogram is better than urinary pregnancy test to detect pregnancy?

**Answer:**

**Why Ultra-sonogram (USG) is better than Urinary Pregnancy Test (UPT):**  
1. **Direct visualization:** USG visualizes the gestational sac as early as 4–5 weeks and detects fetal pole and fetal cardiac pulsation (cardiac motion can be seen at around 6 weeks), confirming a viable intrauterine pregnancy.  
2. **Identification of location (Ectopic pregnancy):** USG accurately detects ectopic pregnancy, whereas UPT (immunological test for hCG) only detects hCG hormone and cannot differentiate between normal intrauterine and ectopic pregnancy.  
3. **Differentiation from abnormal gestation & pathology:** USG differentiates true pregnancy from molar pregnancy (hydatidiform mole), blighted ovum, pseudocyesis, or pelvic tumors/cysts. UPT may show false-positive or abnormally high levels in conditions like hydatidiform mole and choriocarcinoma.  
4. **Fetal viability and multiplicity:** USG confirms fetal viability (life of fetus) and determines single or multiple gestation (e.g. twins), which cannot be determined by urinary tests.  
5. **Age & EDD estimation:** USG accurately estimates gestational age and expected date of delivery by measuring crown-rump length (CRL), biparietal diameter (BPD), femur length (FL), and abdominal circumference (AC).

### Question 01: Define and classify antidote.

**Answer:**

**Antidote:**  
Antidotes are substances which prevent action of poison or neutralize poison or produce signs and symptoms opposite to those produced by poison.

**Classification of antidotes:**  
1. **Physical or mechanical antidote:**  
   * *Mechanism of action:* Prevent action of poison mechanically, without destroying or inactivating damaging actions of poisons.  
   * *Examples:*  
     * Demulcents e.g. egg albumin, starch, barley water, or milk.  
     * Adsorbents e.g. activated animal charcoal.  
     * Diluents e.g. water, milk, or similar drinks.  
     * Bulky food e.g. boiled rice or boiled vegetables.  

2. **Chemical antidote:**  
   * *Mechanism of action:* These substances disintegrate and inactivate poisons by undergoing chemical reaction with them.  
   * *Examples:*  
     * Weak non-carbonate alkalies for acids.  
     * Weak vegetable acids for alkalies.  
     * Common salt in case of silver nitrate poisoning.  
     * Egg albumin in mercuric chloride poisoning.  
     * Freshly prepared ferric oxide in arsenic poisoning.  
     * $\text{KMnO}_4$ to inactivate opium and other alkaloids.  

3. **Physiological or pharmacological antidote:**  
   * *Mechanism of action:* These substances have their own action on different systems of body, producing signs and symptoms opposite to signs and symptoms produced by poisons.  
   * *Examples:*  
     * Naloxone — for morphine.  
     * Neostigmine — for Datura or hyoscine group of poison.  
     * Barbiturate — for strychnine.  

4. **Chelating agents:**  
   * *Mechanism of action:* These substances act on absorbed metallic poisons. They form chelate with metallic poisons that are freely available in circulation and help their early excretion from body through kidney.  
   * *Examples:*  
     * Dimercaprol / British Anti-Lewisite (BAL).  
     * Ethylene-diamine tetra-acetic acid (EDTA).  
     * Penicillamine (cuprimine).  
     * Desferrioxamine.  

5. **Serological antidote:**  
   * *Example:* Anti-snake venom serum (AVS) available for snake bite poisoning of both cobra and viper types.

### Question 09: How can you say the sample collected from scene of crime containing human blood?

**Answer:**

To determine that a sample collected from a scene of crime contains **human blood**, the following step-by-step examination protocol is followed:

1. **Confirmation that the stain is blood:**  
   * **Physical tests:** Naked eye, microscopic examination, and spectroscopic examination.  
   * **Chemical / Screening tests:** Benzidine test (gives blue color), Phenolphthalein/Kastle-Meyer test (deep pink color), Leucomalachite green test, Orthotolidine test.  
   * **Confirmatory microchemical crystal tests:**  
     * *Takayama test:* Pink, feathery crystals of hemochromogen.  
     * *Teichmann test:* Brownish-black rhombic crystals of hemin/hematin chloride.

2. **Determination of human origin (Species differentiation):**  
   * **Precipitin test (Ring test):**  
     * *Principle:* Human blood serum contains proteins in colloidal suspension. When injected into an animal, the animal develops antibodies (antihuman precipitin serum). When human serum comes into contact with this antiserum, precipitins react with human proteins to form a visible precipitate.  
     * *Technique:* A 1:100 saline extract of the bloodstain is placed in a small test tube held in a slanting position, and two drops of antihuman antiserum are added.  
     * *Result:* Formation of a **white ring with well-defined borders at the junction of the two fluids** within 10 to 30 minutes confirms that the blood is of **human origin**.  
   * **Other sero-immunological tests:** Antiglobulin consumption test, mixed antiglobulin test, gel electrophoresis, or ABAcard test.  
   * **DNA Profiling / Typing:** Highly specific confirmation of human origin.

### Question 04: Mention the principle of management of poison.

**Answer:**

**General principles of treatment and management of acute poisoning:**  
1. **Removal of the patient/victim from the source of exposure:** Especially in cases of volatile or gaseous poisons [5].  
2. **Removal of unabsorbed poison:**  
   * *Contact poisoning:* Remove clothes and wash affected skin with lukewarm soap-water [6].  
   * *Ingestion:*  
     * From mouth: Wash mouth [6].  
     * From stomach: Emesis, stomach wash / gastric lavage [6].  
     * From intestine: Purgatives or high colonic lavage [6].  
   * *From site of injection (e.g. snake bite):* Place ligature/splint proximal to site, squeeze or suction [6].  
   * *Intra-vaginal route:* Vaginal douching with plain water [6].  
3. **Dilution of poison and delaying its absorption:** By giving water to drink or bulky bland food [6].  
4. **Elimination of absorbed poison:**  
   * *Diuresis:* Forced alkaline/acid diuresis [6, 7].  
   * *Diaphoresis:* Using pilocarpine or neostigmine [6].  
   * *Dialysis:* Hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis [6-8].  
   * *Chelating agents* and *hemoperfusion* [6, 8].  
5. **Use of specific antidotes:**  
   * Physical/mechanical, chemical, physiological/pharmacological, chelating agents, or serological antidotes (AVS) [9].  
6. **Symptomatic and supportive treatment:**  
   * *Safeguard respiration:* Clearing airway, endotracheal intubation, oxygen, artificial respiration [9].  
   * *Maintenance of circulation:* Treatment of shock, cardiac massage, epinephrine, IV fluids [9].  
   * *Correction of fluid and electrolyte imbalance* [9].  
   * *Control of convulsions:* Barbiturates, diazepam [9].  
   * *Control of body temperature:* Management of hyperthermia (cold sponging) or hypothermia (warm coverings) [9, 10].

### Question 07: How will you manage a case of snake bite?

**Answer:**

**Management of a case of snake bite:**  

* **A. First aid at the site:**  
  1. **Reassure the victim:** Allay anxiety and fear [17, 18].  
  2. **Immobilization:** Immobilize the bitten limb with a splint or sling (do not walk or move the limb) [17, 18].  
  3. **Pressure immobilization:** Apply firm pressure bandage over the bitten limb (especially for Cobra and Krait bites; not recommended for Viper bite) [17, 18].  
  4. **Avoid harmful methods:** Do NOT apply tight arterial tourniquets, do NOT make incisions, suck, or apply chemicals/herbs [17, 19].  
  5. **Immediate transfer:** Rapidly transport to nearest hospital [17].  

* **B. Hospital management:**  
  1. **Resuscitation (ABC):** Maintain clear airway, adequate breathing, and circulation [17].  
  2. **Clinical & laboratory assessment:**  
     * Observe for neurotoxic signs (ptosis, broken neck sign, respiratory paralysis) [20, 21].  
     * Observe for hemotoxic signs (spontaneous bleeding, swelling, blister formation) [20].  
     * **20-Minute Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT):** Incoagulable blood confirms viperine bite (venom-induced consumption coagulopathy) [22].  
  3. **Anti-Snake Venom (AVS) Therapy:**  
     * *Indications:* Presence of systemic envenomation (neurotoxicity, spontaneous bleeding, incoagulable blood, shock, or local swelling involving more than half of the limb) [21, 23].  
     * *Type:* Polyvalent antivenom (effective against Cobra, Krait, Russell's viper, Saw-scaled viper) [23].  
     * *Dose & Administration:* Initial dose is **10 vials** (diluted in 100 mL normal saline or 5% dextrose water) infused intravenously over 40–60 minutes [24].  
  4. **Supportive Treatment:**  
     * Tetanus toxoid prophylaxis and broad-spectrum antibiotics [20].  
     * For neurotoxic respiratory paralysis: Neostigmine with atropine, and mechanical ventilatory support if needed.  
     * For shock and oliguria: IV fluids, treatment of acute kidney injury.

### Question 09: Why fingerprint is called 100% data of identification?

**Answer:**

Fingerprint system (dactylography) is regarded as **100% data of identification (absolute method without any error)** due to the following reasons [21]:  
1. **Permanence:** Ridge patterns are present from birth on both the epidermis and dermis, remain constant throughout the lifetime of an individual, and cannot be altered except by total destruction of the true skin (dermis) [22, 23].  
2. **Absolute individuality (Uniqueness):** No two fingers or hands in the world have identical ridge patterns, not even in uniovular (identical) twins [22, 23].  
3. **Mathematical impossibility of duplication:** According to Galton and Quetelet’s calculations, the chance of two individuals having identical fingerprints is estimated to be **1 in 64 billion** [23, 24].  
4. **Standard of proof:** Comparison and matching of **10 to 12 points of fine characteristics** (minutiae) between prints is universally accepted in courts of law as complete and indisputable proof of identity [22].

### Question 10: How can you say the sample collected from scene of crime containing human blood?

**Answer:**

To prove that a sample collected from the scene of crime contains **human blood**, the following standard laboratory procedure is conducted [25]:

1. **Step 1: Confirmation that the stain is Blood:**  
   * **Preliminary / Chemical screening tests:** Benzidine test (produces intense blue color), Phenolphthalein / Kastle-Meyer test (produces deep pink/permanganate color), or Orthotolidine test [25].  
   * **Microchemical crystal tests (Confirmatory):**  
     * *Takayama test:* Produces pink, feathery crystals of hemochromogen [25].  
     * *Teichmann’s test:* Produces brownish-black rhombic crystals of hemin (hematin chloride) [25].  
   * *Spectroscopic examination:* Shows characteristic absorption bands of hemoglobin derivatives [25].

2. **Step 2: Confirmation of Human Origin (Species differentiation):**  
   * **Precipitin test (Ring test):**  
     * *Principle:* Human blood serum contains proteins that act as antigens. When injected into a rabbit, it produces specific antibodies (antihuman precipitin serum) [26].  
     * *Procedure:* A 1:100 saline extract of the bloodstain is placed in a small, narrow test tube, and two drops of antihuman precipitin serum are layered over it [26].  
     * *Positive finding:* Formation of a **sharp, white precipitate ring with well-defined borders at the junction of the two fluids within 10 to 30 minutes** conclusively proves that the blood is of **human origin** [26].  
   * **Advanced serological and molecular confirmation:**  
     * Antiglobulin consumption test, gel electrophoresis, or ABAcard test [26].  
     * **DNA profiling / typing:** Conclusive and infallible confirmation of human origin [27].

### Question 09: Why stomach wash is contraindicated in corrosive poisoning?

**Answer:**

**Reason:**  
Stomach wash is contraindicated in corrosive poisoning because:  
1. Corrosives cause extensive necrosis, softening, erosion, and chemical destruction of the mucous membrane, submucosa, and muscular coats of the esophagus and stomach wall.  
2. Introducing a stomach wash tube exerts mechanical pressure on these severely damaged and friable walls, which can lead to **fatal mechanical perforation of the esophagus or stomach**.  
3. Perforation precipitates catastrophic chemical mediastinitis, peritonitis, massive hemorrhage, and sudden neurogenic shock/death.  

*(Exception: **Carbolic acid (Phenol)** is an exception among corrosives where stomach wash is indicated using olive oil, liquid paraffin, or castor oil, because carbolic acid fixes and hardens/tans the mucosa and acts as a local anesthetic, minimizing the danger of perforation).*

### Question 02: Define Dactylography. What is its principle?

**Answer:**

**Dactylography / Fingerprint system / Dermatoglyphics / Galton system:**  
Dactylography is the process of taking impression of papillary or friction ridges of finger tips, for the purpose of identification of a person.  
*(Or, Dactylography is the study of finger impressions and is based on the principle that the skin of the balls of the fingers and thumbs is covered with ridges and grooves, the pattern of which varies between individuals and makes absolute identification possible).*

**Basic Principle of Dactylography:**  
The fingerprint system is based on two fundamental, scientifically established principles:  
1. **Permanence:** Ridge patterns appear in intrauterine life (between the 3rd and 4th month of gestation), remain unchanged throughout the lifetime of an individual, and cannot be altered except by total destruction of the true skin (dermis).  
2. **Absolute individuality (Uniqueness):** No two fingers or hands in the world have identical ridge patterns, not even in uniovular (identical) twins. The mathematical probability of two identical finger impressions is estimated to be 1 in 64 billion.

### Question 08: Classify and differentiate hymen.

**Answer:**

**Classification / Types of Hymen:**  
1. **Annular hymen:** Most common form; circular or oval ring-like fold with a central round/oval opening.  
2. **Semilunar or Crescentic hymen:** Crescent-shaped fold situated along the posterior border of the vaginal introitus with a concave anterior edge.

8. **Fleshy / Cartilaginous hymen:** Thick, tough, resistant, and elastic fold.

**Differential Criteria (How to differentiate types):**  
* **Number of apertures:** Single (annular, semilunar), double (septate), multiple (cribriform), or absent (imperforate).  
* **Margin characteristics:** Smooth and regular (annular, semilunar) vs. irregular and fringed (fimbriated).

### Question 10: How will you collect blood from living person for identification of blood group?

**Answer:**

**Method of collecting blood from a living person for blood grouping:**  
1. **Consent & Verification:** Obtain informed written consent from the person and verify identity (photo ID, thumb impression, signature).  
2. **Site Selection & Asepsis:**  
   * Clean the selected site (antecubital fossa for venipuncture, or lateral aspect of the tip of the middle/ring finger for capillary blood) thoroughly with alcohol or spirit and allow it to dry completely.  
3. **Venipuncture Collection:**  
   * Apply a tourniquet lightly on the upper arm.  
   * Using a sterile, dry disposable syringe and needle, draw **5 mL of venous blood**.  
4. **Distribution into Vials:**  
   * **Vial 1 (EDTA / Anticoagulant vial):** Dispense 2–3 mL of blood into a vial containing EDTA or sodium citrate (for red cell suspension and forward grouping).  
   * **Vial 2 (Plain / Clotted vial):** Dispense the remaining 2–3 mL into a clean, dry, sterile plain glass tube without anticoagulant (to allow clotting and separation of clear serum for reverse grouping).  
5. **Bloodstain on Filter Paper:** Place several drops of fresh whole blood onto a clean, sterile piece of white filter paper / gauze cloth, dry at room temperature away from heat or sunlight, and pack separately.  
6. **Labeling, Sealing & Forwarding:** Clearly label the containers with the person's full name, case reference number, date, time, and doctor's signature; seal properly with an official wax seal and dispatch under a proper chain of custody to the forensic serology laboratory.

### Question 10: According to rule of inheritance of MN system blood groups, how can you exclude alleged father?

**Answer:**

**Inheritance of MN blood group system:**  
The MN system is based on co-dominant M and N antigens [9, 10]:  
* Blood group **M** has genotype **MM** [9, 10].  
* Blood group **N** has genotype **NN** [9, 10].  
* Blood group **MN** has genotype **MN** [9, 10].  

**Rule of Exclusion:**  
An antigen (M or N) cannot appear in the child unless it is present in at least one of the parents [11]. Therefore, an alleged father can be conclusively **excluded** in the following conditions:

1. **When child possesses an antigen absent in both mother and alleged father:**  
   * If the child is **M (MM)** or **MN** and the mother is **N (NN)**, the child must have inherited the **M gene** from the biological father. Any alleged father who is **N (NN)** (possessing no M gene) is **conclusively excluded**.  
   * If the child is **N (NN)** or **MN** and the mother is **M (MM)**, the child must have inherited the **N gene** from the biological father. Any alleged father who is **M (MM)** (possessing no N gene) is **conclusively excluded**.  
2. **When the alleged father cannot pass an obligatory antigen to the child:**  
   * If the alleged father is **M (MM)**, all his biological children **must inherit an M gene** (i.e. child must be M or MN). If the child is **N (NN)**, the alleged father is **conclusively excluded**.  
   * If the alleged father is **N (NN)**, all his biological children **must inherit an N gene** (i.e. child must be N or MN). If the child is **M (MM)**, the alleged father is **conclusively excluded**.

### Question 10: According to rule of inheritance of ABO system blood groups, how can you exclude alleged father?

**Answer:**

**Genetics of ABO blood group system:**  
The ABO system is governed by three allelic genes: **A, B, and O**. Genes **A and B are co-dominant**, while **O is recessive**.  
* Group A = Genotype **AA** or **AO**  
* Group B = Genotype **BB** or **BO**  
* Group AB = Genotype **AB**  
* Group O = Genotype **OO**  

**Rules of Exclusion of Alleged Father:**  
1. **First Rule (Classical exclusion):**  
   * An agglutinogen/antigen (A or B) **cannot appear in the blood of a child unless it is present in the blood of at least one of the parents**.  
   * If a child has **antigen A** and the mother is **group O**, the child must have received the A gene from the biological father. Any alleged father who is **group O or group B** is **conclusively excluded**.  
   * If a child has **antigen B** and the mother is **group O**, the child must have received the B gene from the biological father. Any alleged father who is **group O or group A** is **conclusively excluded**.  

2. **Second Rule (Mating / Direct parentage exclusion):**  
   * A parent of **group AB** cannot produce a child of **group O** (because an AB parent must pass either an A or a B gene to the offspring). Thus, if the child is **group O**, an alleged father of **group AB is excluded**.  
   * A parent of **group O** cannot produce a child of **group AB** (because an O parent can only contribute an O gene). Thus, if the child is **group AB**, an alleged father of **group O is excluded**.

### Question 08: Describe the principle of management of a case of poisoning.

**Answer:**

**General principles of treatment and management of acute poisoning:**  
1. **Immediate Resuscitation (Airway, Breathing, Circulation - ABC):**  
   * Clear oral airway, suction of secretions, endotracheal intubation if unconscious.  
   * Oxygen administration or assisted mechanical ventilation if breathing fails.  
   * Maintain blood pressure with IV fluids, vasopressors, and treat cardiac arrhythmias.  
2. **Removal of the victim from exposure:**  
   * Evacuate from toxic atmospheres in gaseous/volatile inhalational poisoning (e.g. carbon monoxide).  
3. **Removal of unabsorbed poison (Decontamination):**  
   * *Skin contact:* Strip off contaminated clothing and thoroughly wash skin with soap and running water.  
   * *Ingestion:* Induce emesis (if conscious and non-corrosive) or perform **gastric lavage / stomach wash** within 3–4 hours (using appropriate lavage solutions or water).  
   * *Gastrointestinal adsorption:* Administer **activated charcoal** (1 g/kg) to adsorb toxins.  
   * *Purgation:* Administer saline cathartics (sodium sulfate) or bowel irrigation.  
4. **Use of specific antidotes:**  
   * Administer physical, chemical, physiological/pharmacological antidotes (e.g. Atropine and Pralidoxime for OPC; Naloxone for Morphine), or chelating agents (e.g. BAL, EDTA for heavy metals).  
5. **Elimination of absorbed poison:**  
   * Forced alkaline diuresis (for salicylates, barbiturates) or forced acid diuresis.  
   * Extracorporeal elimination: Hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or charcoal hemoperfusion [13].  
6. **Symptomatic and supportive treatment:**  
   * Control convulsions with IV Diazepam.  
   * Maintain fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base balance.  
   * Regulate body temperature (cooling for hyperpyrexia; warming for hypothermia).  
   * Prevent secondary infections with systemic antibiotics.

### Question 09: Why Ultra-sonogram is better than urinary pregnancy test?

**Answer:**

**Advantages of Ultrasonography (USG) over Urinary Pregnancy Test:**  
1. **Positive vs. Probable Sign:**  
   * **USG is a positive and conclusive sign of pregnancy** that visually demonstrates the presence of a gestational sac, fetal pole, and embryonic structures.  
   * The urinary pregnancy test (immunological detection of hCG) is only a **probable sign** of pregnancy.  
2. **Direct Confirmation of Fetal Life & Viability:**  
   * USG can directly detect **embryonic cardiac pulsation and fetal heart movements** as early as the 6th week of gestation.  
   * A urinary test merely detects hCG excretion and cannot differentiate between a live fetus, missed abortion, or intrauterine fetal demise.  
3. **Detection of Ectopic Pregnancy:**  
   * USG accurately localizes whether the gestational sac is intrauterine or extrauterine (ectopic pregnancy). A urinary pregnancy test is positive in ectopic pregnancy but cannot pinpoint location.  
4. **Elimination of False Positives:**  
   * Urinary pregnancy tests can yield false-positive results in conditions with elevated hCG or cross-reacting proteins (such as hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma, testicular tumors, lutein cysts, or post-menopausal women). USG easily differentiates these pathologies by their characteristic ultrasonic patterns.  
5. **Accurate Assessment of Gestational Age & Multi-fetal Gestations:**  
   * USG accurately determines gestational age (crown-rump length, biparietal diameter) and detects multiple pregnancies (twins, triplets), blighted ovum, and gross congenital anomalies, none of which can be evaluated by a urinary test.  
6. **Elimination of False Negatives / Hook Effect:**  
   * Urinary tests may show false-negative results if urine is dilute, tested too early, or beyond 16–20 weeks when maternal urinary hCG levels decline. USG remains definitive throughout pregnancy.

### Question 10: A mutilated right upper limb (from mid arm to forearm and hand) was found in the scene of bomb blast injury. From this how can you estimate the sex and height of the victim?

**Answer:**

From an isolated, mutilated right upper limb (mid-arm to forearm and hand), the sex and height (stature) are estimated as follows:

* **A. Estimation of Sex:**  
  1. **Morphological and Physical Examination:**  
     * *General musculature:* Robust, thick, heavily developed muscular bulk indicates a **male**; slender, delicate musculature with abundant smooth subcutaneous fatty tissue indicates a **female**.  
     * *Forearm contour:* Anteroposteriorly flattened in **males**; roundish and smooth in **females**.  
     * *Wrist:* Rough, thick, with prominent bony landmarks, veins, and tendons in **males**; smooth, roundish, slender, and delicate in **females**.  
     * *Hair distribution:* Coarse, dense, thick hair on the forearm and dorsum of the hand favors **male**; fine, sparse, downy, or absent hair favors **female**.  
     * *Hand & Nails:* Larger, broader palms and rugged, blunt nails indicate **male**; smaller, narrower hands, tapered fingers, and manicured/colored nails indicate **female**.  
     * *Personal articles & ornaments:* Presence of bangles, feminine rings, bracelets, or mehndi/henna marks strongly suggests **female**.  
  2. **Osteometric Examination (Bones of upper limb):**  
     * *Radius and Ulna:* In males, they are significantly longer, heavier, thicker, and rougher; diameter of the radial head > 24 mm in males (< 21 mm in females); circumference of the radial head > 69 mm in males (< 55 mm in females).  
     * *Humerus (lower segment):* Robust epicondyles and greater articular surface width in males.  
  3. **Laboratory / Microscopic & Genetic Sexing:**  
     * *Sex chromatin:* Examination of intact epidermal cells or hair root bulbs for **Barr bodies** (present in 20–30% of female cells; absent/rare in males).  
     * *Davidson bodies:* Polymorphonuclear leukocytes from effused blood clots show drumstick nuclear appendages in females.  
     * *DNA analysis (Amelogenin gene):* Definitive sex determination (XY in male, XX in female).

* **B. Estimation of Height (Stature):**  
  1. **Forearm Length Rule:**  
     * The length of the forearm from the tip of the olecranon process to the tip of the middle finger equals **$\frac{5}{19}$ of the total stature**.  
       $$\text{Total Stature} = \text{Length of forearm (olecranon to middle fingertip)} \times \frac{19}{5}$$  
  2. **Length of Long Bones (Multiplication Factors / Regression Equations):**  
     * The intact **radius** or **ulna** is measured on an osteometric board.  
     * Standard multiplication factors or Trotter and Gleser's regression formulae for upper limb bones are applied to calculate total stature (e.g., length of radius multiplied by its corresponding stature ratio factor).  
  3. **Hand Length Proportion:**  
     * Total stature is approximately **9 times the length of the hand** (measured from the distal skin crease of the wrist to the tip of the middle finger).

### Question 01: Define antidote.

**Antidote:**  
Antidotes are substances which prevent the action of a poison, neutralize a poison, or produce signs and symptoms opposite to those produced by a poison [1].

### Question 06: Why teeth becomes chalky white in sulphuric acid ingestion.

**Answer:**

**Reason:**  
In sulphuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) ingestion, teeth become **chalky-white, soft, and brittle** because:  
* Sulphuric acid chemically reacts with the **calcium phosphate and calcium carbonate** present in dental enamel and dentin [10, 11].

### Question 09: What are the precautions you will take before introduction of stomach wash tube?

**Answer:**

**Precautions before introducing a stomach wash tube:**  
1. **Rule out Absolute Contraindications:** Ensure the patient has not ingested corrosive acids/alkalis (except carbolic acid) to avoid fatal esophageal or gastric perforation [19].  
2. **Secure the Airway:** In comatose, drowsy, or uncooperative patients, perform **cuffed endotracheal intubation first** to prevent fatal pulmonary aspiration of stomach contents [19].  
3. **Convulsant Risk:** Do not pass the tube in a patient convulsing from stimulant poisons (e.g., strychnine); control spasms with IV diazepam first [19].  
4. **Remove Oral Obstructions:** Remove artificial loose dentures, chewing gum, or food boluses from the oral cavity [20].  
5. **Insert a Mouth Gag:** Firmly position a wooden or rubber mouth gag between the jaws to prevent the patient from biting the tube, damaging their tongue, or biting the physician's fingers [21, 22].  
6. **Correct Positioning:** Place the patient in the **left lateral position or prone position with the head lowered over the edge of the table**, keeping the mouth below the larynx to prevent fluid from draining into the trachea [23].  
7. **Proper Measurement & Lubrication:** Measure the insertion distance (incisors to epigastrium: ~50 cm in adults, ~25 cm in children) and lubricate the tube tip with glycerin or liquid paraffin [24].  
8. **Verify Placement:** Before infusing lavage fluid, confirm the tube is in the stomach (not trachea) by auscultating over the epigastrium while injecting air, or by submerging the outer end under water (absence of bubbling synchronous with respiration) [24].
