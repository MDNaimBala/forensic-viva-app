### **Question 01:** Define death. Mention the manner of death.

**Answer:**

**Definition of Death:**
In most countries of the world including Bangladesh, there is no clear-cut definition about death in law [1]:
* **Clinically:** Death is defined as "Complete and irreversible cessation of life" [1].
* **Medico-legal definition of death:** Death is permanent and irreversible cessation of functions of the three interlinked vital system of the body (the tripod of life) namely the nervous system, respiratory system and circulatory system [1].
* **Dictionary definitions:**
  * *Black's Law Dictionary:* "The cessation of life; the ceasing to exist" [1].
  * *Chamber's twentieth century dictionary:* "Extinction of life" [1].
  * *Oxford dictionary:* "The end of life" [1].

**Manner of Death:**
* **Definition:** Manner of death indicates the circumstances under which the person died. Or, Manner of death indicates the design how cause of death has come into action [1].
* **Types of manners of death:**
  * **A. Natural:** If death occurs exclusively from disease [1].
  * **B. Unnatural/violent:** If death occurs exclusively from injury or is hastened due to injury in a person suffering from natural disease [1]. Violence may be:
    * Suicidal [2]
    * Homicidal [2]
    * Accidental [2]
    * Undetermined or unexplained [2]

### **Question 02:** Define abrasion. Mention its types.

**Answer:**

**Definition of Abrasion:**
* **Abrasion:** Abrasion is damage or destruction of superficial epithelial covering of skin (epidermis) or mucus membrane, due to impact (mostly frictions) with hard, blunt and rough object or weapon [3].
* *Or,* Abrasion is temporary loss of superficial epithelial covering of skin (epidermis) or mucus membrane, due to forcible friction of body surface against hard, blunt and rough object or weapon [3].

**Types / Classification of Abrasion:**
1. **Scratches / linear abrasion:** Caused by horizontal or tangential friction by pointed end of weapon [4, 5]. Wider at starting than at ending; denuded epithelium heaps up at the end [5].
2. **Grazes / sliding abrasion (or brush burn or gravel rash):** Caused by horizontal or tangential friction between epithelium and some rough surface of an object, ground or weapon [4, 5]. Wider and deeper at the beginning; heaping of epithelium is present at the end [5]. Most commonly in RTA [5].
3. **Pressure abrasion:** Caused by more or less perpendicular force on the body surface [4, 5]. Friction is less and is directed towards the depth of the body [5]. Examples include ligature mark in hanging or strangulation, nail marks in throttling, and teeth bite marks [5].
4. **Imprint / impact or patterned abrasion:** Design and shape of weapon which causes it is left [4, 5]. Weapon of offence can be guessed from it (e.g., tyre mark in RTA) [5].

### **Question 04:** What are the causes of death due to hanging?

**Answer:**

**Immediate Causes of Death in Hanging:**
1. **Asphyxia:** Ligature forces root of tongue against posterior wall of pharynx, and folds epiglottis over entrance of larynx to block airway [6]. A weight of 15 Kg is required to block trachea [6].
2. **Apoplexy / venous congestion:** Jugular veins can be blocked with only pressure of 2 Kg on neck which stops cerebral circulation, causes rapid rise of venous pressure in head and unconsciousness [6].
3. **Combination of asphyxia and apoplexy:** Most common cause [6].
4. **Cerebral anemia or ischemia:** Pressure on large arteries of neck stops flow of blood in them (cerebral anemia and immediate coma) [6].
5. **Reflex vagal inhibition (i.e., vagal shock):** Carotid sinus gets stimulated when irritated by compression and causes vagal inhibition of heart, causing it to stop [6].
6. **Fracture-dislocation of 2nd and 3rd cervical vertebra:** Leading to injury to medulla and upper part of spinal cord, as in case of judicial hanging [6].

*(Delayed causes include: Infections, aspiration pneumonia, edema of lungs/larynx, hypoxic encephalopathy, infarction of brain, cerebral abscess, and cerebral softening) [7].*

### **Question 05:** What do you mean by coup lesion and contre-coup lesion?

**Answer:**

* **Coup Lesion:**
  * **Meaning:** Coup (blow, impact) means that injury is located beneath the area of impact, and results directly by the impacting force [8]. Effects are immediate, resulting in contusions and hemorrhage [8].
  * **Features:**
    * Usually occurs when the head is fixed [8].
    * Smaller the impact area the greater is the likelihood of a coup injury [8].
    * *Example:* If head is fixed (e.g., when lying on the ground) and there is violent impact over the occiput, the fracture and underlying brain damage will be located beneath the site of impact [8].

* **Contre-Coup (Contrecoup) Lesion:**
  * **Meaning:** Contrecoup lesion means that lesion is present in an area opposite to the side of impact [8].
  * **Features:**
    * Usually occurs when head is free / not supported [8].
    * Situated on the contralateral side of the impact area [8].
    * Subdural or subarachnoid hemorrhage may be caused as a contre-coup lesion [8].
    * Sustained in falls and RTAs when moving head decelerates suddenly by hitting a hard surface [9].
    * *Mechanism:* Caused when moving head is suddenly decelerated by hitting a firm/hard surface [9]. Sudden arrest of head results in brain injury which is still in motion, striking the arrested skull [9]. Also, formation of cavity or vacuum in cranial cavity on the opposite side of impact exerts a suction effect which damages the brain [9].
    * *Example:* When a person falls upon the back of his head striking the ground, he may sustain brain injury at the site of impact, i.e., occipital lobes (coup injury), and a more pronounced injury to the frontal lobes (contre-coup injury) [9].

### **Question 04:** Define laceration. Mention its types.

**Answer:**

**Definition of Laceration:**
* **Laceration (tear / rupture):** Lacerations are tears or splits of skin, mucous membranes, muscles or internal organs produced by application of blunt force to a broad area of the body which crushed or stretched tissues beyond limits of their elasticity.

**Types / Classification of Laceration Wound:**
1. **Split lacerations:** Occurs due to crushing of skin and sub-cutaneous tissue by hard blunt weapon against bony surface. It is usually found on areas of body which have subcutaneous bones like scalp, shin of tibia and ulnar border of forearm.
2. **Stretch lacerations:** Caused by forceful frictional impact by hard blunt and rough objects; over-stretching of skin and sub-cutaneous tissue may cause laceration with flapping of skin etc. It is usually seen in run-over motor vehicles.
3. **Avulsion / shearing lacerations:** In this type of laceration there is separation of skin from underlying tissue by shearing force delivered at an acute angle. Such laceration is caused by vehicles in RTA.
   * *Flaying:* Shearing and grinding force by a weight (e.g. lorry wheel passing over a limb) may produce separation (avulsion) of skin from underlying tissues over a relatively large area.
4. **Cut laceration:** This is caused by not-so-sharp edged heavy or moderately heavy instruments, which cause a deep and wide cut over body tissues. They are often seen in chop wound.
5. **Tears:** Tearing of skin & tissues can occur from impact by or against irregular or semi-sharp objects, such as door handle of car. May be caused by blows, broken glass or fall over rough projected object.

### **Question 06:** What are the effect heat and cold to the body?

**Answer:**

**Effects of Exposure to Extreme Heat:**
1. **General effects:**
   * a. Heat stroke / heat hyperpyrexia (rectal temperature $>41^\circ\text{C}$ with delirium, convulsions, coma).
   * b. Heat exhaustion / heat collapse / heat syncope (peripheral circulatory collapse without increase in body temperature).
   * c. Heat cramps / miner’s cramps (painful spasms of muscles due to water and salt depletion).
   * d. Sunburn / heat rash.
2. **Local effects:**
   * a. Burn (dry heat injury).
   * b. Scald (moist heat injury).

**Effects of Exposure to Extreme Cold:**
1. **General effects:**
   * a. Hypothermia (body temperature fall $<35^\circ\text{C}$, ending in cardiac arrhythmias, ventricular fibrillation, coma, and failure of vital centers).
   * b. Immersion syndrome / cold shock.
2. **Local effects:**
   * a. Frost bite (infarction, blistering, and necrosis of peripheral digits, nose, ears).
   * b. Trench foot / immersion foot (due to prolonged exposure to damp cold above freezing point).

### **Question 07:** Mention the types of intracranial haemorrhage.

### **Question 08:** Mention the differential diagnosis of cadaveric spasm.

**Answer:**

**Differential Diagnoses of Cadaveric Spasm (Conditions Simulating Rigidity / Muscle Stiffening):**
1. **Rigor mortis:** Gradual stiffening of muscles following molecular death, preceded by a phase of primary flaccidity, involving all muscles progressively.
2. **Heat stiffening (Heat coagulation):** Rigidity caused by exposure to high temperatures ($>65^\circ\text{C}$) due to denaturation and coagulation of muscle proteins.
3. **Cold stiffening (Freezing):** Stiffening produced when the body is exposed to freezing temperatures ($<4^\circ\text{C}$) due to frozen fluids and fats in tissues.
4. **Gas stiffening:** Stiffening produced by the accumulation and distension of putrefactive gases within body tissues during decomposition.

### **Question 09:** During autopsy, from lung findings how can you say it was a case of live birth?

**Answer:**

**Autopsy Lung Findings Indicating Live Birth (Respirated Lungs):**
1. **Volume:** Enormous increase in volume; expanded lungs completely cover the pericardial sac / heart.
2. **Weight:** Weight of lungs is doubled (approximately $1/35$ of total body weight, compared to $1/70$ in stillborn).
3. **Margin:** Margins are rounded and blunt (rather than sharp).
4. **Consistency:** Soft, spongy, elastic, and crepitant to touch (air-filled vesicles crackle on pressure).
5. **Color:** Mottled or marbled in appearance (pale pink / light red), rather than uniformly dark reddish-brown.
6. **On Sectioning:** Abundant frothy blood exudates from the cut surface on gentle pressure.
7. **Hydrostatic Test (Raygat’s Test):** Whole lung and all its cut pieces (12 to 20 pieces) float buoyantly in water, even after firm compression to expel tidal air (due to retained residual air).

### **Question 10:** How can you explain the blast lung in case of bomb blast injury?

**Answer:**

**Blast Lung:**
* **Definition:** Specific pulmonary injury caused by the air blast is called **BLAST LUNG** [25].

**Mechanism and Explanation:**
* An air blast produces a very high-pressure **shock wave** which spreads concentrically from the center of detonation at approximately the speed of sound (1120 feet/sec) [26, 27].
* When this shock wave passes through the body, it causes serious disruption and damage to **inhomogeneous air-containing tissues** (especially the lungs), particularly if overpressure exceeds **700 kPa (100 lb/in²)** [27, 28].
* The alternating compression and decompression wave causes stress waves and shear waves across the alveolar-capillary interfaces [27, 28].

**Autopsy / Pathological Findings:**
1. **Sub-pleural patchy hemorrhages**, characteristically appearing in the line of the ribs [27].
2. **Rupture of alveolar septa** with extensive intra-alveolar and intra-pulmonary hemorrhages [27, 29].
3. **Sub-pleural bullae** formation along the pulmonary margins [27].
4. **Desquamation** of alveolar and bronchial epithelium (seen lying free in the air spaces) [27].
5. **Reactive pulmonary edema** and blood-stained froth filling the airways, which may lead to secondary bronchopneumonia if the patient survives [27].

### **Question 03:** What do you mean by lucid interval in case of head injury?

### **Question 07:** What are the factors affecting electric burn?

**Answer:**

**Factors Affecting Electric Burn / Effects of Electric Injury:**
The effects and severity of the burn produced by the passage of electric current depend on:
1. **Voltage:** High voltage causes severe electrical and flame burns; low voltage typically causes localized contact burns (joule burns).
2. **Amperage (Strength of current):** Determines the severity of shock and heat generated ($I = V/R$).
3. **Type of Current:** Alternating current (AC) is 4 to 5 times more dangerous than direct current (DC) and induces tetanic muscle spasms that prevent release.
4. **Resistance Offered by the Body:** The greater the resistance, the more heat is produced ($H = I^2Rt$):
   * Dry, thick, calloused skin offers high resistance ($100{,}000\text{ to }1{,}000{,}000\text{ ohms}$), resulting in prominent local joule burns.
   * Wet, moist, or sweating skin offers low resistance ($1{,}000\text{ ohms}$), allowing systemic current passage with minimal or absent surface burns.
5. **Duration of Contact / Length of Exposure:** Prolonged contact produces deep charring, craters, and extensive tissue coagulative necrosis.
6. **Surface Area of Contact:** Small points of contact concentrate heat density (producing deep punched-out joule burns); broad contact areas disperse current with minimal local burns.
7. **Direction / Pathway of Current:** Transverse pathway (arm-to-arm) or vertical pathway (hand-to-foot) passes through the thorax, endangering the heart and respiratory muscles.
8. **Preparedness and General Health:** Anticipation of shock, presence of underlying cardiovascular disease, or intoxication alter individual susceptibility.

### **Question 09:** How will you manage a case of 10% burn?

**Answer:**

**Management of a Case of 10% Burn:**

* **A. First Aid:**
  1. Remove the patient immediately away from the source of burning.
  2. Clothing should be removed gently.
  3. Cool the burned area with continuous irrigation of tap water (not cold/ice water) for 20 minutes.

* **B. Definitive Hospital Management:**
  1. **Hospital Admission / Care:** Admit the patient in a clean, isolated burn unit to prevent infection.

     * Cleanse the wound gently with sterile normal saline.
     * Apply topical antibacterial cream (e.g., 1% silver sulfadiazine cream).
     * Apply sterile, non-adherent dressing.
  6. **Infection Control & Prophylaxis:**
     * Administer tetanus toxoid (and tetanus immunoglobulin if unimmunized).

### **Question 05:** What are the factors affecting electrocution?

**Answer:**

**Factors Affecting the Severity and Effects of Electrocution:**
1. **Voltage:** Low voltage (<250 V) predominantly causes ventricular fibrillation; high voltage (>1000 V) produces extensive electrodermal burns, charring, and central respiratory arrest [15].
2. **Amperage (Current intensity):** Most important physiological determinant of lethality ($I = V/R$); currents of 70–100 mA produce fatal ventricular fibrillation [16].
3. **Type of Current:** Alternating current (AC) is 4 to 5 times more dangerous than direct current (DC) because it causes sustained tetanic muscle spasms that prevent the victim from releasing the conductor ("hold-on" effect) [16].
4. **Resistance of the Body:**
   * Dry, thick, calloused skin offers high resistance ($100{,}000\text{ to }1{,}000{,}000\ \Omega$), producing marked localized Joule burns [16].
   * Wet, sweating, or submerged skin offers low resistance ($1{,}000\ \Omega$), allowing fatal current flow with little or no cutaneous burn [16].
5. **Duration of Contact:** Prolonged contact allows deep tissue destruction and extensive Joule burns [16].
6. **Pathway of Current:** A pathway traversing the heart (e.g., hand-to-hand or left hand-to-foot) causes cardiac arrest or ventricular fibrillation; a pathway through the brainstem causes respiratory center paralysis [15, 16].
7. **Surface Area of Contact:** Small contact areas concentrate current density causing deep, punched-out crater burns; broad contact areas diffuse heat [16].
8. **Individual Health & Susceptibility:** Presence of preexisting heart disease, intoxication, or emotional shock enhances fatality [16].

### **Question 10:** How can you explain the blast lung in case of bomb blast injury?

**Mechanism of Production:**

   * **Colliquative putrefaction:** Soft tissue liquefaction and bursting open of cavities (5–10 days).

2. **Adipocere Formation (Saponification):**

### **Question 07:** Mention the causes of death due to drowning.

**Answer:**

**Causes of Death in Drowning:**
1. **Asphyxia:** Due to inhalation and aspiration of fluid filling the bronchial tree and alveolar spaces, blocking air exchange.
2. **Ventricular Fibrillation / Acute Heart Failure:** In **fresh water drowning**, massive hemodilution (blood volume increases by up to 50–72%) with hemolysis, hyperkalemia, and hyponatremia precipitates fatal ventricular arrhythmias within 4–5 minutes.
3. **Severe Pulmonary Edema & Hypoxia:** In **salt water drowning**, hypertonic water draws fluid out of circulation into alveolar spaces, causing severe pulmonary edema, hemoconcentration, and death from anoxia within 8–12 minutes.
4. **Laryngeal Spasm (Dry Drowning):** Inrush of water into the nasopharynx or larynx triggers intense reflex laryngeal spasm, closing the airway without water entering the lungs (10–20% of cases).
5. **Vagal Inhibition (Immersion Syndrome / Hydrocution):** Cold water striking the surface of the body, epigastrium, or entering the ear/nasal passages stimulates mucosal nerve endings causing reflex vagal cardiac arrest.
6. **Exhaustion:** Physical exhaustion while struggling in water.
7. **Trauma / Secondary Injuries:** Striking submerged rocks, harbor pilings, or boat propellers causing fatal skull fracture or visceral rupture.
8. **Secondary Drowning Complications:** Hypoxic encephalopathy, adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), bronchopneumonia, or metabolic acidosis hours to days after rescue.

* **Immediately / Fresh:** **Red** (due to fresh, oxygenated hemoglobin) [13].

* **4th day:** **Bluish-black to brown** (due to conversion into **hemosiderin**).
* **5th to 6th day:** **Greenish** (due to breakdown into **hematoidin**).
* **7th to 12th day:** **Yellow** (due to oxidation into **bilirubin**).
* **2 weeks (14 days):** **Completely cleared**; skin returns to its normal color.

*(Note: The color transformation always progresses centripetally—starting at the outer periphery and advancing towards the center)*.

### **Question 10:** A baby was brought to emergency department by his mother with the complaint of lacerated injury in right shoulder. In addition to that doctor found multiple injuries of different ages in different part of body? What will be your diagnosis in such a case?

**Answer:**

**Diagnosis:**
* **Battered Baby Syndrome (BBS)** *(also known as **Caffey’s Syndrome**, **Child Abuse Syndrome**, or **Maltreatment Syndrome**)* [15].

**Justification for the Diagnosis:**
1. **Injuries in Different Stages of Healing:** Coexistence of fresh trauma (fresh shoulder laceration) alongside older injuries of different ages (e.g., multi-colored bruises, healing abrasions, or old fractures) is the hallmark of repeated, non-accidental trauma [15].
2. **Multiple Anatomical Sites:** Involvement of multiple, disparate body regions incompatible with a single accidental fall [15].
3. **Discrepancy in History:** The mother’s single complaint (isolated shoulder trauma) fails to explain the diffuse, widespread multi-age lesions discovered on clinical examination [15].

### **Question 04:** Define adipocere formation. Mention its importance.

**Answer:**

**Definition of Adipocere Formation (Saponification):**
* **Adipocere Formation / Saponification:** Adipocere formation or saponification is the formation of a soft, whitish, crumbly, waxy, and greasy material occurring in the fatty or fat-containing tissues of a dead body [6].
* *Or,* It is a modification of the putrefactive process resulting from the conversion of unsaturated liquid fats into saturated solid fatty acids (palmitic, stearic, and hydroxystearic acids) through bacterial fat-splitting enzymes (primarily *Clostridia*) in a moist, anaerobic environment [6].

**Medico-Legal Importance of Adipocere Formation:**
1. **Surest sign of death** [6].
2. **Estimation of time since death:** Provides a rough estimate of the post-mortem interval (commences in 1–3 weeks, takes about 3 months to complete) [6].
3. **Preservation of individual identity:** Facial contours and bodily features remain intact and preserved for years or decades, permitting personal identification [6, 7].
4. **Preservation of wounds and injuries:** Ante-mortem trauma, cuts, stab wounds, ligature furrows, and firearm marks remain well preserved and recognizable [6, 7].
5. **Indicates the environment of disposal:** Indicates that the body was immersed in water, buried in moist soil, or kept in a damp, poorly ventilated grave [6].

### **Question 05:** Mention the causes of death due to electrocution.

**Answer:**

**Causes of Death in Electrocution:**
* a. **Ventricular fibrillation or cardiac arrest without fibrillation:** Common in arm-to-arm or left arm-to-leg current pathways traversing the heart.
* b. **Paralysis of the respiratory center:** Occurs when the electric current passes through the head or brainstem.
* c. **Respiratory muscle paralysis with secondary asphyxia:** Occurs when low-voltage current produces prolonged tetanic spasms of the intercostal muscles and diaphragm.
* d. **Extensive electrodermal burns and shock:** Caused by high-voltage currents producing massive tissue coagulative necrosis and secondary hypovolemic/burn shock.
* e. **Delayed death:** Occurs hours to days later from severe metabolic acidosis, acute tubular necrosis (myoglobinuria), or secondary wound infection/sepsis.

### **Question 08:** Mention the character of entry wound of distant shot rifled firearm.
